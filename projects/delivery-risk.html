PROJECT 02 · SUPPLY CHAIN ANALYTICS

Predicting delivery risk.

From 180,519 historical orders to a machine-learning system that estimates the risk of late delivery before action is required.

APPROACH

We analysed delivery performance, identified the strongest signals behind late deliveries, tested different Machine Learning approaches and integrated the final model into a decision-support application.

CONTEXT Supply Chain Analytics
DATA

180,519 orders · 45 variables

STACK

Python · Pandas · Scikit-learn · FastAPI · Streamlit

01 / THE PROBLEM

Late delivery is not just a KPI.

A late order becomes a problem before it appears in the final delivery report.

The project started from a large historical order dataset containing information about customers, products, commercial conditions, markets and delivery configuration.

The objective was not simply to measure how many deliveries were late. We wanted to understand whether the available order information could be used to identify risky orders earlier.

This led to a more proactive question: can we estimate delivery risk before the delay actually happens?

THE DATASET

180,519 orders analysed.

45 Original variables
98,977 Orders classified as late-risk
54.8% Overall late-delivery risk
4 Shipping modes
03 / DATA EXPLORATION

First, we needed to understand the signal.

The first analyses showed that delivery risk was strongly connected to the shipping configuration.

We compared the late-delivery rate across the different shipping modes and tested the relationship statistically using a Chi-square test.

SHIPPING MODE First Class

95.3% late-delivery risk in the historical dataset.

SHIPPING MODE Second Class

76.6% late-delivery risk in the historical dataset.

STATISTICAL TEST χ² = 37,716

The Chi-square test produced a p-value effectively equal to zero, indicating a strong association between shipping mode and late-delivery risk.

04 / LOOKING BEYOND THE OBVIOUS

What happens when we remove shipping configuration?

Shipping mode clearly dominated the first model. We therefore tested a second approach without delivery‑configuration variables to understand which business characteristics were carrying additional information.

01 Full model

Shipping mode and scheduled delivery time included in the feature set.

02 Remove configuration

Delivery‑configuration variables removed to neutralise their dominance.

03 Rebuild the model

Random Forest retrained on the remaining business variables only.

04 Interpret the drivers

Feature importance grouped into clear business dimensions.

05 / BUSINESS SIGNALS

The strongest remaining signals were commercial.

After removing delivery-configuration variables, commercial characteristics became the dominant business dimension in the model.

The Random Forest feature importance analysis highlighted Order Item Discount, Discount Rate, Sales, Order Item Quantity and Product Price among the strongest numerical variables.

01 · COMMERCIAL 67.95%

Combined feature importance attributed to the Commercial business dimension.

02 · PRODUCT 12.88%

Product-related variables represented another meaningful part of the signal.

03 · CUSTOMER 10.74%

Customer segment characteristics also contributed to the prediction.

06 / MACHINE LEARNING

From analysis to prediction.

We tested Logistic Regression, Decision Tree and Random Forest models using a preprocessing pipeline combining categorical encoding and numerical scaling.

Random Forest achieved an F1-score of approximately 0.665 on the main feature set. More importantly, the model could transform the characteristics of an individual order into a probability of late delivery.

01 · BASELINE Logistic Regression
F1 · 0.662

Baseline classification model used to establish the first predictive benchmark.

02 · NON-LINEAR Decision Tree
F1 · 0.664

Non-linear model tested to capture more complex relationships between order variables.

03 · SELECTED MODEL Random Forest
F1 · 0.665

Selected model used for the operational prediction pipeline.

MODEL INTERPRETATION

Understanding what drives the prediction.

After selecting Random Forest, we analysed feature importance to understand which business variables contributed most to the prediction of late-delivery risk.

The analysis showed that commercial variables represented the strongest business dimension in the model, followed by product and customer characteristics.

KEY INSIGHT Commercial characteristics were the strongest remaining drivers of delivery risk.
PYTHON / RANDOM FOREST FEATURE IMPORTANCE

Feature importance analysis showing the variables contributing most to the model's late-delivery prediction.

FROM MODEL TO APPLICATION

A prediction is useful only when it can be used.

The final step was to move the model outside the notebook and integrate it into an application that can analyse new orders.

01 Upload data

New order data can be introduced through the application.

02 FastAPI

The API receives the order information and sends it to the trained model.

03 Prediction

The Random Forest estimates the probability of late delivery.

04 Decision support

The probability is translated into a risk level and an operational recommendation.

08 / RISK FRAMEWORK

Probability becomes an operational signal.

Instead of asking users to interpret a raw probability, the application converts the prediction into four operational risk levels.

LOW < 40%

No immediate intervention is required.

MEDIUM 40–59%

Keep the order under monitoring.

HIGH 60–79%

Monitor closely and consider proactive intervention.

CRITICAL ≥ 80%

Immediate intervention is recommended.

MODEL OUTPUT Probability

The underlying model continues to produce a numerical late-delivery probability.

BUSINESS OUTPUT Recommendation

The application turns the prediction into an action-oriented message.

09 / DECISION SUPPORT

Machine Learning predicts. The agent interprets.

The Machine Learning model answers: “What is the risk?”

The decision-support layer answers: “What should we do about it?”

01 Order characteristics

Commercial, product, customer and delivery information.

02 ML prediction

Estimated probability of late delivery.

03 Risk level

Low, Medium, High or Critical.

04 Recommendation

A clear indication of the level of intervention required.

PROJECT OUTCOME

From historical delivery data to proactive risk management.

The project moved from descriptive analysis of delivery performance to a predictive decision- support system. Historical data was used to identify delivery-risk signals, a Machine Learning model was trained to estimate risk for new orders, and the model was integrated into an application that translates predictions into operational recommendations.

The result is not simply a prediction model. It is a workflow designed to help users identify which orders deserve attention and prioritize intervention before a delivery problem becomes visible.
